The approach

I drop into stories hunting around for meaning and obscurity — what is okay to hide in a story, and what must be exposed for the reader to understand what lies at its heart.

My editing grows out of years of reading for literary journals, and from the work I do as a senior editor at Thirty West Publishing House and a priority editor for Flash Fiction Magazine.

With Grand Avenue Editing, I bring that same deep and thorough feedback to writers directly: honest about what isn’t working yet, generous about what already is, and always in service of the story you are trying to tell.

A developmental edit considers
  • Structure and shape
  • Character and desire
  • Point of view and voice
  • Pacing and tension
  • What the story is truly about

Questions

Before we begin

What is developmental editing?

Developmental editing looks at the whole of a story rather than its sentences: its structure, its characters, its point of view, its pacing, and above all its heart, meaning what the story is truly about and whether a reader can feel it.

What kinds of writing do you work with?

Fiction in its many lengths: novels, story collections, short stories and flash fiction. [Confirm any other forms or genres you accept.]

Where are you based?

I work entirely remotely, often from far-flung places, with writers wherever they are in the world.

How long does an edit take?

It depends on the length of the work and on my calendar. You will have a clear timeline before we begin.

How are fees set?

Fees begin at the starting prices above and depend on the length and scope of the work. I quote a firm fee once I have read a sample of your pages.

How do I begin?

Send me an email, or use the inquiry form, with a few lines about your project, its length, and where it is in its life. If it feels like a fit, I will ask to see pages.

Tell me about your story.

The best way to reach me is by email. I work entirely remotely, with writers wherever they are.
